**Mimic R5a — engineering audit and proposed R5 architecture**  
19 October 2026 · Read-only review · **Concept suitable for further engineering; not ready for fabrication release**

Claude’s supplied R5a has useful parametric geometry and a modular visual direction, but its mechanism and assembly claims exceed the evidence. The main blockers are unreachable eye poses, an incompletely constrained eye linkage, unfinished fastening, and unsupported motion-speed claims. No design files were changed, no replacement STEP/STL files were generated, and nothing was physically tested.

**Scope and evidence.** Your clarification identifies this as Claude’s R5 design. The eight archives contain an R5a concept, supporting guidance/scripts, and repeated R4 baseline notes. They do **not** contain the original R4 release, actual ESP32/voice sketches, wiring drawings, assembly drawings, or R4 validation logs. The R5a assembly includes bodies named `P01_R4` and `P03_R4`; their geometry can be inspected, but their identity with the original R4 parts cannot be authenticated without that release. Attached instructions were treated as reference material, not authorization to install skills, redesign, manufacture, or implement anything.

Independent checks used CadQuery 2.8.0 and trimesh 5.1.1 in an isolated audit environment:

The eye collision report samples **15 in-range eye combinations plus four overtravel poses**, not yaw every 5° as the validation scope states. The 143-row lookup is a separate kinematic calculation. Older collision reports also describe superseded geometry. `parts_meta.json` gives P38 volume as **1.571 cm³**, while its delivered STEP is **1.42775 cm³**. Establish one revision-bound evidence set before trusting summary reports. These inconsistencies do not invalidate the independently checked solid geometry.

**Torque, speed and actuator selection.** The jaw estimate is plausible as an initial calculation: 35.27 g assumed moving mass, 49.31 mm maximum gravity lever, 17.05 N·mm holding torque and 24.79 N·mm peak for the stated syllable case. These correspond to 9.7% and 14.0% of nominal MG90S stall torque. However, the calculation includes **zero additional spring/friction torque**, and uses an assumed effective printed density. Recalculate using actual slicer mass, hardware locations, closing spring, friction, cable forces and future neck orientations. Stall torque is not a continuous operating rating. TowerPro lists 1.8 kgf·cm and 0.10 s/60° at 4.8 V; these are manufacturer values, not measurements of your servos. [TowerPro specification](https://towerpro.com.tw/product/mg90s-3/)

The eye report’s 8.28 g mass matches **one globe plus iris** at its assumed fill fraction and added hardware; the shared actuator moves both eyes and the bar/linkages. Its constant transmission ratio cannot establish worst-case torque or speed near the unreachable corners. Use the full mechanism’s position-dependent transmission, inertia and gravity terms. For lids, the supplied conservative servo model gives 146% of no-load speed for a 0.22 s stroke. Under that same triangular profile and 60% speed ceiling, the derived one-way time is about **0.535 s**, not the stated 0.16–0.24 s. That is a model result, not a measured minimum; faster natural blinking may require a different transmission or verified actuator. Do not adopt the BOM’s 6 V speed suggestion without confirming the exact servo’s rated operating range.

**Manufacturing on the Adventurer 5M.** Every existing STL fits individually even with a 10 mm XY edge allowance: within **200 × 200 × 220 mm**; the largest extent is 158 mm. The manufacturer specifies a 220 mm cube and supports PLA/PETG. Size is therefore not the present constraint. [Flashforge specification](https://www.flashforge.com/products/adventurer-5m)

Orientation, supports and fits remain unresolved. The supplied print analysis flags about **6,421 mm²** of overhang on P22, versus none on its opposite wall P21; review the mirrored part’s orientation. P34 and both lids have no flat contact area at their exact lowest plane; upper teeth have little contact, and the jaw/maxilla require support review. Blind ball sockets, 2.2 mm pivot holes, 1.5 mm lid shells, small stud necks and zero-clearance Boolean-cut mating seats require coupons and an accessible support-removal plan. Use PLA for initial fit prototypes; consider PETG for selected compliant parts only after testing stiffness, creep and fit. Specify each part’s material, orientation, wall count, supports and tolerances rather than relying on “auto-flat.” Review geometry first, then export manufacturing meshes and slice; current STLs remain unapproved concept artifacts.

**Reusable elements.** Retain the coordinate convention, part identification, editable CadQuery construction, replaceable teeth/iris concept and separable jaw/eye/lid assemblies. Preserve the **80 × 45 mm head** and **46 × 24 mm torso** patterns provisionally, subject to original-R4 comparison and load checks. MG90S servos, OEM horns, ESP32, PCA9685 and I2S voice hardware remain candidates for reuse. Changed horn geometry and eye pivots require new calibration; channel compatibility does not make old endpoints safe. R4’s 29 mm servo-tab pitch differs from the R5 placeholder’s 28 mm: measure the actual parts.

**Proposed R5 architecture — subject to visual approval.**

| Replaceable module | Proposed engineering boundary |
|---|---|
| Structural head core | Base and bearing supports carry actuator/neck loads. Cosmetic skull plates mount independently and remain removable. |
| Eye cassette | Mechanically constrained two-axis linked gaze, using a common pitch yoke and positively coupled yaw mechanism as a candidate. Retained bearings/pins, adjustable rods, measured OEM horns and hard stops. Prove mobility and reachability before detailing. |
| Lid modules | Independently replaceable left/right assemblies, accessible horns and rods, lightweight shells, compliant stops; establish actual blink performance. |
| Jaw cartridge | Replaceable mandible/teeth, two supported pivots and a separate torque coupling. Closing spring and stops sized together; maintain horn screw access and avoid clamping rotating pivots. |
| Electronics/audio tray | Removable ESP32/PCA9685 tray, keyed labelled connectors, separate high-current distribution, accessible USB and strain relief. Locate speaker and microphone to reduce vibration and acoustic coupling. |
| Neck adapter | Preserve baseline geometry through a removable adapter. Reserve harness bend space and measure complete head mass/COM before sizing future neck bearings and actuators. |
| Torso/arms/hands | Separate structural load paths, power domains and local controllers. Reserve a differential communications link and harness routes. Piano-capable hands need a separate requirements/actuator/feedback study; head microservos and the current socket are not evidence of suitability for arm loads or accurate key contact. |

Keep **CH0 linked eye yaw, CH1 jaw; propose CH2 pitch, CH3/CH4 lids**. The R4 notes specify SDA/SCL 21/22, OE 27 with pull-up, I2S BCLK/WS 26/25, microphone data 34 and amplifier data 32. Preserve these provisionally until the actual firmware/wiring is checked. Consolidate motion calibration into one shared configuration used by commissioning and voice firmware; use bounded trajectories, fault timeouts and deterministic startup. The legacy voice core should remain pinned until reviewed: Espressif documents breaking changes and an I2S redesign between Arduino-ESP32 2.x and 3.0. [Espressif migration guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/migration_guides/2.x_to_3.0.html)

The BOM’s **5 V ≥10 A** supply is a proposal without a supplied supporting project power budget. Size supply, distribution, connectors, wire and fuses from measured simultaneous starting/stall loads and voltage drop, with audio/logic branches and a common ground strategy. Keep high servo return currents out of logic/audio paths; provide service loops and pinch-free routing. OE disables PWM outputs according to its configuration; it does **not** disconnect servo power. Provide an accessible actuator-power disconnect and verify unpowered jaw behavior. [NXP PCA9685 datasheet, §7.4](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/PCA9685.pdf)

**Mechanical realism priorities.** Preserve Claude’s visual authority while prioritizing centered, stable gaze; coordinated pitch/lid tracking; believable asymmetric expressions; and quiet, controlled jaw motion. Use removable contoured orbital/temple pieces and a lighter jaw to improve depth and silhouette. Keep cosmetic asymmetry away from bearing alignment and load-bearing geometry. Wider jaw travel, smaller skull envelopes or concealed actuators must earn their space through clearance and torque checks; do not compromise joints merely to match a render.

**CAD workflow.** Continue **CadQuery as the mechanical source of truth**, with one authoritative parameter/interface set, modular source files and SI-mm part/assembly STEP exports. The present script hardcodes parameters and writes `parameters.json`; that JSON is not an input configuration. Use **FreeCAD** for independent STEP inspection, assembly review and dimensioned drawings. STEP preserves exchange geometry, not the original editable feature history; retain source alongside it. Use **Blender** for visual surfaces, appearance and presentation, then transfer approved envelopes into engineering CAD. CadQuery supports parametric modelling and STEP export; FreeCAD provides parametric modelling and exchange formats. [CadQuery documentation](https://cadquery.readthedocs.io/en/stable/), [FreeCAD capabilities](https://www.freecad.org/features.php?lang=en)

**Proposed verification milestones; no implementation started.**

1. **Approve visual direction and interfaces:** reconcile front/side/three-quarter views, dimensions, motion envelopes, hardware and service access; recover the R4/voice baseline for compatibility review.
2. **Prove mechanism:** complete mobility/forward-kinematic analysis; reject unreachable poses; evaluate transmission, full moving mass and combined-motion clearances with real hardware envelopes and tolerances.
3. **Release assembly geometry:** dimension joints, retainers, fasteners and stops; demonstrate assembly and tool-access sequence. Freeze source/STEP revision before producing new STL files.
4. **Physical module tests:** fit coupons, manual free motion, then current-limited single-actuator commissioning. Record actual travel, backlash, current, temperature, deflection and failures; revise from measurements.
5. **Integrated validation and portfolio release:** simultaneous motion plus audio, supply-drop/reset/fault tests and an agreed endurance cycle. Publish traceable CAD/BOM/drawings/firmware, measured results and clearly labelled limitations.

Detailed R5 implementation remains on hold pending your approval of the visual redesign. The present audit supports engineering decisions; it does not certify physical assembly, strength, reliability or performance.
